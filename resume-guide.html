How to Write a Resume as a Fresher

A practical guide to creating your first resume, presenting your skills, showcasing projects, and applying for internships and entry-level jobs.

What should a fresher's resume include?

A resume is a concise summary of your education, skills, projects, experience, and achievements relevant to a job. If you have no formal work experience, you can still present coursework, academic projects, internships, volunteering, and independent work.

Tailor your resume to the role you want. A software internship, accounting role, and marketing position may require different skills and examples.

1. Use these essential resume sections

Contact information

Include your name, professional email, phone number, city, and relevant portfolio or LinkedIn link. Avoid unnecessary personal information such as identification numbers or passwords.

Career objective or summary

Write two or three specific sentences explaining the role you are seeking, relevant strengths, and what you can contribute. Avoid generic claims without evidence.

Education

List your degree or qualification, institution, expected graduation date or completion year, and relevant grades when appropriate. Keep dates and details accurate.

Skills

Include relevant technical skills, software, languages, and communication abilities that you can demonstrate. Match them to the job description honestly.

Projects and experience

Describe academic projects, internships, freelance work, volunteering, or independent projects. Explain what you built or did, which tools you used, and the result where you can substantiate it.

Certifications and achievements

Include relevant completed courses, certifications, awards, competitions, or leadership activities. Do not list qualifications you have not earned.

2. How to write a career objective

Avoid objectives such as "I want a challenging job in a reputed company." Instead, connect your current abilities with the position you want.

Example: Computer science fresher

"Computer science graduate with hands-on experience building responsive web pages using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Seeking an entry-level web development role where I can contribute to practical projects and continue developing my front-end skills."

Example: Marketing fresher

"Recent business graduate with experience in academic market research, presentation development, and content planning. Seeking an entry-level marketing role to support campaigns and learn through practical work."

Use these as starting points and adapt them to your actual education, experience, and target position.

3. How to describe projects without work experience

A well-described project can demonstrate practical skills. Use this simple structure:

  • Project: What you created.
  • Tools: Technologies or methods used.
  • Contribution: What you personally did.
  • Result: A verifiable outcome, if available.

Example: Student toolkit website

Technologies: HTML, Bootstrap, JavaScript

Developed a responsive student resource website featuring percentage and CGPA calculators and a browser-based resume builder. Published the static website using Cloudflare Pages.

Example: Academic data analysis project

Analysed a dataset for an academic project, cleaned the data using the tools actually used, and summarised findings in charts and a written report.

Only use project descriptions that accurately reflect your own work. Do not invent clients, results, or achievements.

4. How long should your resume be?

For many freshers, one page is a practical starting point. Focus on relevance and readability rather than filling space. A longer resume may be appropriate when substantial relevant experience or a specific application requires it.

Formatting tips

  • Use clear headings and consistent spacing.
  • Choose a readable font and text size.
  • Use bullet points for projects and experience.
  • Keep dates, job titles, and formatting consistent.
  • Save and review the final PDF before sending it.
  • Follow the employer's requested file format.

5. Common resume mistakes to avoid

  • Spelling mistakes and grammatical errors.
  • Sending the same untailored resume to every role.
  • Listing skills you cannot explain or demonstrate.
  • Inventing work experience, certifications, or results.
  • Using unnecessary graphics that make text hard to read.
  • Including sensitive personal information that is not needed.
  • Sharing a resume with broken links or incorrect contact details.
